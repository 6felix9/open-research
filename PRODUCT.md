# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Hackathon participants at any stage of the funnel: the solo hacker making a decision the evening before, a small 2-4 person team aligning on scope in the days leading up, and serial hackers who treat ideation as a recurring research practice. All are technically literate; none need hand-holding. They arrive with stakes (a submission deadline, prize money, sponsor relationships) and limited time.

## Product Purpose

Open Research is an agentic ideation tool for hackathons. It maps sponsor incentive structures, reads tech interest signals, scans prior art, and produces ranked ideas with rationale, prior-art citations, and a concrete demo moment. The output is not a brainstorm — it is an opinionated shortlist, ordered by fit, with enough supporting reasoning that the user can commit.

## Brand Personality

Curious, generous, warm. The tool shows its work without making a performance of it. It feels like a research partner who has already done three hours of reading before you sat down — and hands you the summary with genuine enthusiasm for your problem. It does not feel like software.

References: Linear (precision, speed, confident defaults) and Are.na (quiet, curatorial, unhurried, generous with context). The overlap: a tool that respects the user's intelligence, doesn't inflate, and lets the work speak.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS (white backgrounds, Inter, pastel accents, rounded-corner-friendly UI)
- AI product marketing aesthetic (dark navy, electric blue, gradient glowing orbs, "neural" visuals)
- Consumer productivity apps (bubbly, illustrated, onboarding tooltips, designed for a general audience)

## Design Principles

1. **Show the reasoning.** The log, the prior art citations, the score breakdown — these are the product, not supporting chrome. Surface them without apology.
2. **Opinionated by default.** The tool has already decided something. Rankings are real, defaults are considered, the interface reflects confidence in its own output.
3. **Warm precision.** Dense information, never cold. The interface cares about the user's outcome.
4. **Respect the hacker.** No empty states that explain how the product works. No progress theater. Trust the user to know what a button does.
5. **The demo moment is the whole game.** Every idea has one; the interface should be built with the same conviction — there is a specific moment that sells this, and everything else supports it.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA as the floor: 4.5:1 contrast for body text, keyboard navigable, screen reader compatible for key interactions.
